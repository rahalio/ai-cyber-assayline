# Assayline — User stories

**Product:** [PRODUCT.md](./PRODUCT.md)


### Head of model risk management

- As the head of model risk, I want the estate's growth rate set against my validation capacity for the next four quarters, so that I can put a resourcing decision to the CRO before the queue becomes a supervisory finding.
- As the head of model risk, I want cost per validated model and cycle time reported by tier, so that I can show where industrialisation is actually paying and where it is not.
- As the head of model risk, I want the total explicit conservatism carried by the estate, located and priced, so that I can take a capital-release proposition to the CFO instead of only a cost report.
- As the head of model risk, I want open supervisory add-ons shown with their remediation plans and their measured reduction, so that the function's regulatory liability is managed rather than merely known.

### Validation project-management office lead

- As the PMO lead, I want submissions checked against the published standard before a slot is granted, so that validators stop absorbing incomplete work and the delay lands where it was caused.
- As the PMO lead, I want to allocate slots from a calendar that knows each validator's skills and current load, so that a tier-one replication is not assigned to someone who cannot perform it.
- As the PMO lead, I want to see returned submissions by submitting team and by missing component, so that I can fix a recurring gap at its source rather than by chasing individual submissions.
- As the PMO lead, I want tasks routed onshore or offshore according to a recorded data-sensitivity basis, so that placement is a defensible decision rather than a scheduling convenience.

### Validator

- As a validator, I want the tier's playbook to define exactly which steps are in scope — conceptual soundness, data validation, replication, challenger construction, sensitivity analysis, implementation review — so that scope arguments happen once at tiering rather than repeatedly during the work.
- As a validator, I want to raise a finding that automatically constrains the model's permitted use at the severity I assign, so that my conclusion has an effect without my having to escalate it socially.
- As a validator, I want the platform to refuse to let me validate a model I helped develop, so that independence is a property of the system rather than something I have to remember to declare.

### Model developer and model owner

- As a model developer, I want the completeness standard visible before I submit, with my submission's gaps listed, so that I am not queued and then returned.
- As a model owner in the business, I want to see my model's permitted uses, open findings, and monitoring thresholds, so that I can carry the responsibility for decisions taken on it knowingly.
- As a model owner, I want to request an exception when I need to use a model outside its recorded intent, and to receive a committee decision with an expiry and conditions, so that a legitimate business need does not become an undocumented misuse.
- As a model developer, I want to declare a conservative assumption as an explicit overlay with its rationale, so that my judgement is reviewed and retained rather than being stripped out or hidden.

### Control unit, internal audit and supervisory relations

- As the control unit, I want to own the completeness standard, the tiering criteria, and the appeal path, so that the gate holds when a business escalates against it.
- As an internal auditor, I want to test whether validation depth actually matched the model's tier and whether independence held on every engagement, so that I can audit the process rather than re-perform validations.
- As a supervisory relations lead, I want a pack assembled from the live record showing inventory boundary decisions, tiering rationale, validation evidence, open findings, and quantified model risk against appetite, so that an examination does not consume the function for a quarter.
